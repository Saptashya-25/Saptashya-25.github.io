Working papers

AI at the Fingertips: How Does LLM App Adoption Reshape Smartphone Usage?

Saptashya Ghosh, Saharsh Agarwal

Under review  ·  Information Systems Research

Abstract

The rapid diffusion of large language models (LLMs) is reshaping how users interact with digital technologies. As LLMs become available through dedicated smartphone applications, they may substitute for existing applications or complement them by encouraging broader digital engagement. Using proprietary smartphone usage data from Android users in India and a staggered difference-in-differences design, we document three findings. First, adoption and sustained usage follow different demographic patterns: younger users and men are more likely to adopt, but conditional on adoption, women are more likely to become sustained users. Second, sustained usage concentrates smartphone activity on a smaller set of applications, with lower app diversity persisting for up to 79 weeks after adoption. Third, substitution is concentrated in categories whose tasks overlap with LLM capabilities, namely web browsers, search engines, and writing applications, while social, e-commerce, and other transactional categories remain unaffected. We find little evidence of complementarity. These findings suggest that LLMs are emerging as a new gateway to online information, with implications for market definition, competition policy, and the platforms that depend on search traffic.

Policy papers

Climate Change and Household Finance

Saptashya Ghosh, Sankalp Mathur

India Finance Report 2025  ·  CAFRAL, Reserve Bank of India

Abstract

Climate change presents significant challenges to household finances. Expanding financial access to households is crucial for climate adaptation and resilience. Climate vulnerable areas have seen slower growth in loans such as business and mortgage loans, especially by traditional financial intermediaries, while microfinance loans have shown notable growth. FinTechs have recently stepped in, demonstrating how technology can enhance financial support to households impacted by adverse climate shocks.

Master's thesis

Do Political Cycles Alarm Officers? Evidence from the Indian Bureaucracy

M.S. Economics, Shiv Nadar University  ·  Advised by Arka Roy Chaudhuri

Abstract

Bureaucrats are central to policy implementation, and the decisions they make carry consequences for a wide range of societal outcomes. Yet bureaucratic careers remain exposed to political interference, institutional instability, and corruption, and officers' work is frequently disrupted by political processes. This paper develops an empirical framework for identifying the effect of the electoral cycle on bureaucratic transfers. Using monthly variation in a comprehensive panel of bureaucratic officers spanning 1950 to 2020, we document that transfer decisions are significantly shaped by the timing of elections. Transfers rise sharply in the eleven months following an election relative to the remainder of the electoral cycle.
